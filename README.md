## About

The Stargazer Map is a toolkit designed for reflecting on, analysing, and reporting participatory sessions with children.

It builds on the Stargazer Map framework, developed from a scoping review of 101 methodological and theoretical papers in Child-Computer Interaction. The framework identifies sixteen contextual elements organised into four interconnected nebulae: **Experience**, **Social**, **Structural**, and **Identity**.

Rather than providing a checklist, the Map encourages researchers to explore how different elements influence one another and contribute to the meaning-making process of a participatory session.

The toolkit was developed as part of [Tadaa](https://www.irenezanardi.com/tadaa/).

## Who is it for?

The toolkit is designed for researchers and practitioners working with children in participatory design, co-design, and related research activities. It can be used:

- 🗣️ During debrief sessions
- 🔍 During data analysis
- 👥 In team reflections
- ✍️ When writing up research findings

## What's Included?

The toolkit contains the Map and two sets of cards.

| Material | Contents |
|---|---|
| Stargazer Map | Overview of the framework |
| Nebula cards | 4 cards |
| Contextual-element cards | 16 cards |

### Materials

🗺️ **The Stargazer Map**
A visual overview of the four nebulae and the sixteen contextual elements, used as a shared surface for discussion.

🌌 **Nebula cards**
Four cards introducing the main areas of the framework: Experience, Social, Structural, and Identity.

⭐ **Contextual-element cards**
Sixteen cards, each describing a contextual element and offering prompts to support reflection and discussion.

## How to Use the Toolkit

1. Explore the four nebula cards to familiarise yourself with the framework.
2. Use the contextual-element cards alongside the Map to identify the factors that shaped a session.
3. Discuss how these elements relate to and influence one another.
4. Decide which contextual details should be documented and reported.

The cards can be used individually or collaboratively. In teams, they help surface overlooked dynamics and give structure to reflective conversations.

💡 The Stargazer Map is not a checklist. There is no need to cover every element: the aim is to understand how context shaped the session and what is worth reporting.
